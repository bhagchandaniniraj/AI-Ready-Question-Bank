### Bloom's action verbs

| Level | Suitable action verbs |
|---|---|
| Remember | Define, List, Identify, State, Name |
| Understand | Explain, Describe, Illustrate, Summarize |
| Apply | Apply, Demonstrate, Implement, Use, Show |

| Create | Design, Develop, Formulate, Construct, Propose |

Examples:

- Analyze: Analyze the importance of board governance and explain its major principles.
- Evaluate: Evaluate the effectiveness of different risk mitigation strategies.
- Apply: Demonstrate how an organization can apply access controls to protect sensitive information.

Questions should be:
- Short and concise.
- Easy for students to understand.
- Academically accurate.
- Appropriate for the allocated marks.
- Properly mapped to Bloom's Taxonomy.

**1 Mark**

- No unnecessary explanation.

**4 Marks**
- Approximately four meaningful marking points.
- Definition or introduction.
- Main explanation.

**6 Marks**

- Introduction and explanation.
- Important features or components.
- Practical relevance or example.

**8 Marks**
- Detailed and comprehensive explanation.
- Introduction and definition.
- Main concepts and components.
- Features and characteristics.
- Working or process wherever applicable.
- Advantages, limitations or applications wherever relevant.
- Suitable examples.
- Conclusion.

## 6. AI-FRIENDLY MARKING RUBRICS – HIGHEST PRIORITY

The university uses AI-based answer-sheet checking.

Therefore, marking rubrics must be designed to recognise concepts, keywords, explanations, examples and alternate presentations rather than matching exact sentences.

### A. Mandatory language-tolerance instruction

Every single rubric row must include the following instruction in its Expected Evidence:

**Do not deduct marks for grammar, spelling, punctuation, syntax, sentence construction, minor language mistakes or imperfect English. Evaluate only the student's conceptual understanding, relevant keywords, technical accuracy and correctness of the explanation. Award appropriate marks for correct concepts even when expressed in simple, brief or imperfect language.**

This instruction must be present in every rubric row, not merely in a general instruction outside the rubric.

### B. Liberal marking policy

The rubrics must instruct the AI evaluator to:

1. Award generous partial credit for every correct concept.
2. Recognise relevant keywords even if explanations are brief.
3. Accept equivalent terminology.
4. Accept explanations written in students' own words.
5. Accept bullet points, paragraphs and tables.
6. Accept correctly labelled diagrams and flowcharts.
7. Award marks for relevant practical examples.
8. Recognise correct concepts even when the student does not reproduce the model answer.
9. Avoid demanding identical sentence structures.
10. Avoid deducting marks twice for the same missing concept.
11. Give partial marks for incomplete but relevant understanding.
12. Award the highest justified marks when the essential concepts are demonstrated.

Do not award marks for irrelevant or factually incorrect statements merely to increase scores.

### C. Keyword-based evaluation

Include relevant keywords in the Expected Evidence of each rubric.

For example, for Risk Mitigation:

- Risk identification.
- Risk assessment.
- Risk reduction.
- Preventive controls.
- Corrective controls.
- Security policies.
- Monitoring.
- Risk treatment.

If a student uses an equivalent expression that demonstrates the same concept, award appropriate marks.

Do not insist that every keyword must appear if the concept has otherwise been explained correctly.

### D. Diagram recognition

Where applicable, award marks for:

- Correct components.
- Correct labels.
- Correct sequence.
- Appropriate arrows.
- Logical relationships.
- Correct flow of information.
- Relevant supporting explanation.

A correctly drawn and labelled diagram must receive appropriate marks even if the student provides a shorter written explanation.

### E. Fractional marks

Use fractional marks wherever appropriate.

Permitted examples:

- 0.25
- 0.50
- 0.75
- 1.00
- 1.25
- 1.50
- 2.00
- 2.50

Do not restrict the rubric to whole numbers.

### F. Total marks must match exactly

The sum of all rubric rows must equal the original question marks.

Examples:

| Question marks | Example rubric total |
|---|---:|
| 1 | 1.00 |
| 4 | 4.00 |
| 6 | 6.00 |
| 8 | 8.00 |

Never generate a rubric whose total exceeds or falls below the question marks.

### G. Topic-specific rubrics

Never blindly reuse identical rubrics for all questions.

Prepare criteria according to the actual question.

Each rubric must contain:

- `criterion`
- `expectedEvidence`
- `marks`

The Expected Evidence must cover the main answer, alternate answers, relevant keywords and acceptable diagrams or tables.
